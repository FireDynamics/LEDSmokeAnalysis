Pedestal of the LED search areas, estimated in the far field of the LED strips.

The sum over a search area (sum_col_val) holds, besides the light of the LED, light that does not dim with the LED: the dark level of the sensor where it differs from the optical black the black level is taken from, ambient light, and light that smoke scatters into the line of sight. In dense smoke the LED dims while this pedestal stays, so the plain sum overestimates the transmission and the extinction coefficients saturate.

The pedestal is estimated for every image where no light of an LED is expected, in the far field of the strips:

  1. Two bands run parallel to the strip, one on each side, over +-R along the strip (R = search_area_radius) and from the inner to the outer far-field distance across it. Pixels closer than the inner distance to any LED of any strip are left out, so neighbouring strips do not enter the bands.

  2. The level of a band is the mean of its pixels of the color channel.

  3. The levels of each side are smoothed along the strip, with a running median and a running mean over the LEDs. The pedestal varies slowly along the strip, so this removes the noise of the estimate without changing it: the pedestal corrected sum is as noisy as the plain sum.

  4. The pedestal of an LED is the lower of the levels of its two sides. An object in one of the bands, another light source or the brighter side of a gradient can only raise a level, so the lower side is the safe one.

  5. N times this pedestal is subtracted from the sum of the search area, with N the number of pixels of the color channel in the search area.

Why the far field and not the neighbourhood of the LED: around an LED there is light of the LED itself, the wings of its image and, behind a window or in hot gases, a halo that can reach a hundred pixels and more. A pedestal measured there subtracts transmitted light and raises the extinction coefficients, the more the denser the smoke. The far field holds none of it. Stray light that peaks at the strip is brighter at the LED than in the far field, so the far-field level is a lower bound of the pedestal: the corrected sum lies between the plain sum and the light of the LED in the search area. The bound holds for the dark level and for scattered light. Ambient light on a background that is brighter beside the strip than behind the LED would raise the far-field level above the pedestal of the search area.

What the correction does not do: where the image of an LED widens beyond its search area, the search area loses light of the LED. That error has the opposite sign and is no pedestal; the correction leaves it as it is.

ledsa.data_extraction.farfield_background.build_farfield_geometry(search_areas: ndarray, led_arrays: Sequence[Sequence[int]], img_shape: Tuple[int, int], search_area_radius: int, distances: Tuple[float, float]) → FarfieldGeometry[source]

Determine the pixels of the two far-field bands of each LED and the place of each LED on its strip.

With t the coordinate along the strip and s the coordinate across it, both relative to the center of the search area, a pixel belongs to the band of one side if -R <= t < R and inner <= s < outer, and to that of the other side if -outer <= s < -inner. For a vertical strip these are the rows r-R..r+R-1 and the columns c+inner..c+outer-1 and c-outer..c-inner-1. Pixels outside the image and pixels closer than the inner distance to the center of any search area are left out.

Parameters:

  • search_areas (np.ndarray) – Array with rows (LED id, pixel row, pixel column), the row index equals the LED id.

  • led_arrays (Sequence[Sequence[int]]) – LED ids of each LED strip, as found in step 2.

  • img_shape (tuple[int, int]) – Shape of the image.

  • search_area_radius (int) – Radius R of the search areas.

  • distances (tuple[float, float]) – Inner and outer distance of the bands across the strip in pixels.

Returns:

The band pixels and the place on its strip of every LED.

Return type:

FarfieldGeometry

ledsa.data_extraction.farfield_background.estimate_strip_directions(search_areas: ndarray, led_arrays: Sequence[Sequence[int]], num_neighbours: int = 4) → ndarray[source]

Estimate the local direction of the LED strip at each LED from its nearest neighbours in the same LED array.

The direction is the principal axis of the positions of the LED and its num_neighbours nearest neighbours of the same array. It is oriented such that the row component is positive (or, for horizontal strips, the column component), so the two sides of a strip are the same for all of its LEDs.

Parameters:

  • search_areas (np.ndarray) – Array with rows (LED id, pixel row, pixel column), the row index equals the LED id.

  • led_arrays (Sequence[Sequence[int]]) – LED ids of each LED array.

  • num_neighbours (int) – Number of neighbours used to estimate the direction.

Returns:

Array of shape (number of LEDs, 2) with the unit vector (row, column) along the strip, NaN for LEDs that are not part of an array with at least two LEDs.

Return type:

np.ndarray
